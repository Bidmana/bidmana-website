The Difference

What a checkmark cannot show

Most of the platforms above do one or two things well. GovWin IQ and HigherGov are research products with a pipeline attached. Vultron is a proposal writer. Govly is an opportunity network for OEMs, distributors, and resellers. GovDash, Sweetspot, and CLEATUS cover more of the lifecycle, and each keeps a copy of your library on its own servers to do it.

Bidmana was built around a different idea: the proposal is written by a team, that team is often part consultant and part partner company, and the documents belong to the customer. Everything else follows from that.

The people are in the platform, not just the data

Consultants and teaming partners work inside your proposal under an NDA you control, with hours, approvals, and invoices handled in the same place.

Your library never moves

Past performance and capability documents are read from SharePoint, OneDrive, or Google Drive when a draft needs them. No second copy, no second place to secure.

Drafts are checked, not just generated

Every claim in a draft carries a citation to your own evidence, and a verifier reads the draft back against that evidence before a reviewer sees it.

Built by veterans, for the companies they came from

Bidmana is veteran-owned. Its founders have run captures, written proposals, and secured federal systems from the inside, and built the platform they wished they had.
